# Decisions

This file records every decision that affects results, made in S0 before any result exists. Section 1 follows the list in the spec's S0 section, in its order (1.1 to 1.8), then adds the loader rules, the sign and reference conventions and the statement on shared segments that the data and the spec need (1.9 to 1.11), and the S1 and S2 rules that must be fixed before the fits and experiments run (1.12, 1.13). Section 2 checks the dataset readme's claims against the files. Section 3 lists the data anomalies that matter for S1 and S2, with the rule for each. S2 appends the frozen Q and R tuning here, as the spec requires. Conventions and equations are in `docs/model.md`.

**Where this file departs from the spec.** Each departure is labelled again in the entry named.

Deviations, where the spec says something else:
- 1.1: the OCV comes from the C/20 discharge half only, not from the average of the charge and discharge halves.
- 1.5: the evaluation set is 62 runs, not "every drive cycle at every temperature". Out: `551_HWFET` (no current), `562_Mixed4` to `Mixed8` (partial runs), `602_Mixed4` (corrupt) and the six PausCycl files. The exclusions also decide the offset (1.7).
- 1.7: the current offset is 200 mA, not 100 mA.
- 1.10: the reference SoC follows the cycler's counter and the coulomb-counting baseline integrates the logged current, so the two are not equal by construction. This amends the spec's sentence that they are. Experiment 1 reports "coulomb counting vs reference" as a column.
- 1.12: two fits per HPPC level (R0, R1, tau1 on the discharge pulses and their 40 s rests; R2, tau2 on the 3600 s rest), not one fit "on the pulse plus its relaxation".
- 1.13: experiment 3 starts where the reference reaches 70 % with initial SoC 50 % and 90 %, not where it reaches 50 % with 30 % and 70 %.

Refinements, where the spec is silent or loose:
- 1.1, 1.6 and 1.10: in C20DisCh and Dis_2C files Ah_removed counts from the counter's zero at the step start, not from the first row of the file (the spec: "since the start of the file").
- 1.5 and 1.10: SoC0 is taken from the counter at the first row used, so it is 0.9948 for `582_LA92` and 0.9964 for `571_Mixed6`, not 1.
- 1.7: "round" means a multiple of 50 mA.
- 1.9: dt comes from the Time column (the spec: "from the timestamps"). The gap rule is the spec's exact zero-order hold of the previous kept sample's current over the actual dt, at every step, with no special gap code.
- 1.12: the R0 rule (98 % of the commanded current, within 0.3 s) and the treatment of pulses cut by a voltage limit.

How to read the evidence:
- `[inv]` = `results/data_inventory.md` and `[cap]` = `results/capacity.md`, both written by `python/inventory.py` (`make inventory`). `[chk N]` = section N of `results/data_checks.md`, written by `python/data_checks.py` (`make checks`). Every measured number in this file is in one of these three files, and the bracket names the section or table. Facts about the dataset that the readme states are cited as such.
- Files are named `<folder>/<test>_<descriptor>`; [inv] has the full names. Row numbers count from 0. Current and Ah values quoted from the files are in the cycler's sign (discharge negative) unless a line says otherwise. Differences in SoC are in percentage points (pp).

## 1. Decisions

### 1.1 OCV source

**OCV(SoC, T) comes from the C/20 discharge half of each folder's C20DisCh file only. This deviates from the spec, which takes the average of the C/20 charge and discharge halves.**

- Rule: SoC axis = 1 - Ah_removed / Q_ref, with Ah_removed = -Ah counted from the counter's zero at the start of the discharge step (1.10; this refines the spec's "since the start of the file"). Fit a PCHIP through the discharge half, evaluate it on the 101-point SoC grid (1.8), and check monotonicity. Below the SoC where a temperature's discharge ends, hold the last value and record the valid SoC range per temperature in `results/params.md` (spec, S1). HPPC 1 h rest voltages are plotted over each curve as a cross-check only. The spec's fallback is unchanged: if any cold curve fails the check, use the 25 degC curve at every temperature.
- Reason: the two halves of a C20DisCh file do not share one Ah axis, so any average depends on an alignment rule the data cannot pick. Hysteresis is out of scope, so one branch is used instead of a midpoint whose position depends on that rule. Cost: the table is a discharge-branch OCV and includes the C/20 discharge overpotential. S1's HPPC rest-voltage cross-check shows how large that offset is, and S4 lists it as a limitation.
- Evidence:
  - The charge half puts back more Ah than the discharge half removed, except at -20 degC. Ratio returned / removed, in campaign order: 1.068 (25 degC: 2.9709 Ah against 2.7807 Ah), 1.066 (40), 1.018 (10), 1.034 (0), 1.015 (-10), 0.983 (-20). The cause is unexplained [cap: per-folder table and *Anomalies*; chk 11].
  - Charge minus discharge voltage at 50 % SoC under four alignments A to D (definitions in [chk 11]). At 25 degC: A 7.9, D 69.1, B 37.2 mV; C equals A (7.9 mV) because the discharge Ah equals Q_ref there. At -20 degC: A 148.6, D 130.5, B 140.1, C 148.1 mV. Under A the 25 degC charge curve lies 23.9 mV *below* the discharge curve at 80 % SoC, which a charge curve should not do [chk 11].
  - Monotonicity of the raw discharge half (voltage against Ah): no rises at 40, 25, 10, 0 and -10 degC. At -20 degC there are 51 single-sample rises, the largest 0.34 mV, and the voltage rebounds at most 0.51 mV above the running minimum. A PCHIP through each raw discharge half is monotone on the grid points inside the measured range at all six temperatures (smallest step 1.85 mV, at -20 degC), so the fallback is not expected to trigger [chk 11]. The 6.07 mV dip at -20 degC lies in the CC part of the charge half (75 falls), not in the discharge half [chk 11].
  - On the Q_ref axis the discharge ends at SoC 3.27 / 0.00 / 4.74 / 6.55 / 7.81 / 10.28 % at 40 / 25 / 10 / 0 / -10 / -20 degC [chk 11].
  - Discharge current: median 0.15325 A, or 0.14814 A at 10 degC (test 575, section 2 of this file), logged at 60 s [chk 11; inv: *median dt*].
  - Ah reference point: the counter was reset at the start of the discharge step, and the file opens one 60 s interval later at Ah[0] = -0.00245 to -0.00254 Ah [chk 13]. Counting from the reset is the same reference Q_ref uses (1.6), so the 25 degC curve spans SoC 1 to 0 exactly. Counting from Ah[0] instead would shift the axis by 0.091 % of Q_ref [chk 12].

- **Refinement after the S1 open-loop validation, recorded before the corrected results are produced: the OCV table is the C/20 discharge curve plus a smooth correction that brings it onto the HPPC 1 h rest voltages of the same temperature.**
  - Reason: the C/20 discharge curve sits below the relaxed open-circuit voltage by the overpotential at the C/20 current, which grows at low SoC and when cold. With the uncorrected table the 25 degC sanity gate failed (RMSE 51.84 mV against 30 mV, [results/model_fit.md] of commit 073da3a), most of the error sitting in the final rest below 10 % SoC where the model stays far below the measured rest voltage, and the cold runs start far below the measured voltage. The HPPC rest points are the cross-check this decision already named; they relax from the discharge direction like the C/20 curve, so they carry the same hysteresis side.
  - Rule, per temperature: take the voltage of the last row of each 3600 s rest after a move (at SoC = the counter SoC where the move ended) and the voltage of the last row before the first discharge pulse of level 1 (at SoC = the level-1 pulse SoC); form delta_k = V_rest,k - V_C20(SoC_k); fit a PCHIP through (SoC_k, delta_k) and hold it flat beyond the first and last point; OCV = V_C20 + delta on the grid. dOCV/dSoC and the monotonicity check run on the corrected curve; the fallback rule is unchanged. The correction amounts per level are tabulated in results/params.md and drawn in results/plots/s1/ocv.png. The residual relaxation at the end of the cold rests (1.12, up to 5.90 mV at -20 degC) is a known small bias of the rest points.
  - The sanity gate stays as specified: RMSE over all rows used of the US06 run. results/model_fit.md also reports the RMSE over the evaluation window (SoC_ref >= 10 %, C14) for information. No window is chosen to pass the gate.

### 1.6 Q_ref

**Q_ref = 2.7807 Ah, the 25 degC C/20 discharge capacity from `data/raw/25degC/10-26-18_09.29 549_C20DisCh_25degC_LGHG2.mat`. One value is used at every temperature, for the reference SoC, the OCV axis and the EKF.**

- Reason: this is the spec's definition. The value is the charge removed from the full-charge state. The counter was reset at the start of the discharge step, and the file opens 60 s into it at Ah = -0.00254 Ah [chk 12], so the counter value includes the unlogged first minute. The alternative that uses logged samples only, -(Ah_end - Ah[0]) = 2.7782 Ah, is not used.
- Evidence: the counter reads -2.78074 Ah at the last discharge sample (row 1096, Time 65,733.85 s = 18.26 h, 2.79993 V). Median discharge current 0.15325 A, cell at 23.6 to 24.3 degC [chk 12, 11; cap: row 1].
- Consequences:
  - 25 degC ran first (section 2 of this file), so Q_ref comes from the freshest cell. The 40 degC C/20 capacity, measured eight days later, is already 2.6899 Ah [cap: rows 1 and 6].
  - Cold runs end at a higher reference SoC. The lowest end-of-run SoC_ref is 9.17 / 6.85 / 10.83 / 15.49 / 22.67 / 42.90 % at 40 / 25 / 10 / 0 / -10 / -20 degC [chk 7]. So the spec's window (SoC_ref >= 10 %) cuts only runs at 40 and 25 degC.

### 1.7 Current-offset size (experiment 4)

**The offset is +200 mA, added in the project sign (positive = discharge) to the current fed to the EKF and to the plain coulomb counter. This deviates from the spec's default of 100 mA, which fails the spec's own 5 pp guard.**

- Guard (spec): drift_pp = 100 x dI x t / (3600 x Q_ref) at the end of the shortest evaluation run must be at least 5 pp. If not, raise the offset to the smallest round value that passes.
- Reason: the guard makes the coulomb-counting drift large enough to show against the filter error in every run, including the shortest. The offset is a chosen test value, not a sensor specification. "Round" is taken as a multiple of 50 mA.
- Shortest run: `n20degC/610_US06`, 2760.4 s. All of it lies inside the evaluation window [chk 7].
- 100 mA gives 2.757 pp (fails). The threshold is 181.3 mA. 200 mA gives 5.515 pp and is the smallest multiple of 50 mA that passes [chk 7].
- The size depends on the evaluation set (1.5). Hypothetical: with the six PausCycl files in, the shortest run would be `40degC/562_PausCycl` (792.2 s), with a threshold of 631.8 mA, so 650 mA. With the 562 partial runs instead, it would be `40degC/562_Mixed7` (784.5 s), with a threshold of 638.0 mA, so also 650 mA; with both in, 650 mA [chk 7]. With the 62-run set the offset stays 200 mA.

### 1.9 Filter rate, dt and gap rules

**The filter runs at the native 0.1 s, with no decimation, and dt comes from Time, never from TimeStamp. Gap rule (refines the spec's "use the actual dt"): the predict step uses the spec's exact zero-order hold, the previous kept sample's current over the actual dt, at every step. There is no special gap code, and nothing is interpolated.**

- Loader rules, in the order of `docs/model.md`: (1) drop rows with Voltage <= 0 and rows with NaN in Time, Voltage, Current, Ah or Battery_Temp_degC (Wh is not used); (2) apply the per-file cuts of 1.5 (`571_Mixed6` from row 391; `582_LA92` only the rows dated 26/11); (3) of consecutive rows that share one Time value, keep the last; (4) dt = Time(k) - Time(k-1) between kept rows. Cutting first keeps the same rows, because every NaN row lies in rows the 582 cut removes [chk 5].
- Reason: the drive cycles are logged at 0.1 s, and the spec needs that rate to resolve tau1. TimeStamp is reliable only to about 1 s (two formats, unreliable millisecond digits [inv: legend]), so it is used only to order files and to find hidden pauses. Process noise scales with dt (spec, S2) and the zero-order hold is exact for any dt, so a longer step needs no special code. Interpolating or zeroing the current across a step would add an assumption the data supports no better than the hold.
- Evidence:
  - Rate: median drive-cycle dt is 0.100 to 0.101 s per folder, with 1st to 99th percentiles of 0.094 to 0.106 s [inv: summary; chk 1]. Of the 4,498,539 steps in the 70 drive files, 99.893 % lie within 0.09 to 0.11 s; 4,212 have dt = 0, 4 are negative, 37 exceed 0.2 s and 36 exceed 1 s [chk 1].
  - Duplicates: 5,754 rows share their Time with the previous row, in 177 of 206 files [chk 2]. 47 of the 70 drive files end on such a duplicate, with Current = 0 A on both rows; of the 4,212 duplicate rows in drive files, 4,160 are in `602_Mixed4` [chk 2]. HPPC files have 193 to 247 duplicates each. In 9 to 13 per file the two rows differ (67 pairs; 63 of them are the last sample of a current step), by at most 2.56 mA and 0.170 mV. The 68th differing pair in all files is in `610_HWFET`. So keep-last moves no kept sample by more than that [chk 2].
  - Voltage <= 0: only the last row of `25degC/552_Charge16`, `40degC/557_Charge10` and `0degC/590_Charge16`, an end-of-test sentinel [chk 5; inv: flags].
  - NaN: only in `10degC/582_LA92`: rows 1491, 1492 and 1944 in Voltage, Current, Ah, Wh, Power and Battery_Temp_degC, and row 1943 in Wh only. All four lie in rows the cut in 1.5 removes [chk 4b, 5].
  - Gaps: 10 of 70 drive files have steps longer than 1 s [chk 3]. Nine evaluation runs have 32 cycle-boundary steps of 1.147 to 2.143 s (3 UDDS runs and 6 LA92 runs; the trimmed `582_LA92` keeps 5 of them, 1.641 to 2.056 s). The tenth file is `602_Mixed4`, which is excluded [chk 3].
  - Cost of the hold at the 32 boundary steps. Measured: the held current and the counter's change over the same step differ by at most 0.0024 pp of Q_ref, because the current at these steps is at most 117.5 mA [chk 3]. Theoretical worst case: the longest step, 2.143 s, at the 6.00 A regen cap gives 0.129 pp, and at the largest discharge current in the evaluation runs, 18.10 A, 0.387 pp [chk 3]. The nine runs are accepted with this rule.
  - Why `602_Mixed4` is excluded and not handled by a rule: rows 3867 to 8026 (4,160 rows) are identical at Time 386.704 s, then comes a 415.993 s step in which the counter moves -0.11582 Ah (4.165 pp). Holding the row before (+1.50948 A) moves SoC by +6.27 pp and the row after (-1.49159 A) by -6.20 pp, against -4.17 pp for the counter, and the held-current coulomb counter ends 10.39 pp from the reference over the run. No hold rule reproduces the counter [chk 4c, 7].

### 1.10 Sign conventions, reference SoC and baseline

**I = -Current (positive = discharge). T = Battery_Temp_degC. Units are SI (A, V, s, degC, Ah). SoC is a fraction internally and in pp in reports, and eta = 1. Ah_removed = -(Ah - Ah_start) on the cycler's counter, positive when charge is removed. Three departures from the spec are labelled below: the Ah_start of C20DisCh and Dis_2C files (refinement), SoC0 below 1 for two runs (refinement, 1.5) and a reference SoC that is not identical to the baseline (deviation).**

- Ah_start (refines the spec's "Ah ... since the start of the file"): it is the counter at the first row used, for every other file. C20DisCh and Dis_2C files open one 60 s interval after a counter reset at the step start; there Ah_start = 0, so Ah_removed = -Ah. This matches Q_ref = 2.7807 Ah (1.6) and makes the 25 degC OCV axis end at exactly SoC 0; counting from Ah[0] would shift the axis by 0.091 % of Q_ref [chk 12, 13]. Ah_removed is only formed inside a stretch with no counter reset: drive, Cap_1C, C20DisCh and PausCycl files have no reset, and the HPPC resets come after the pulse train (1.2). Charge, Dis_0p5C and Dis_2C files do reset [chk 16], but no fit uses them; [cap: *Method*] measures their discharge capacities. Dis_0p5C files continue the C/20 counter [chk 13].
- Reference and baseline (deviation: this amends the spec's sentence that coulomb counting equals the reference by construction): the reference is SoC_ref = SoC0 - Ah_removed / Q_ref on the counter, with SoC0 = 1 except `582_LA92` (0.9948) and `571_Mixed6` (0.9964) (1.5). The plain coulomb-counting baseline integrates the logged current with the same dt and hold rules (1.9), as a BMS would. The two differ by at most 0.299 pp (0.30 pp) over an evaluation run, in `0degC/589_US06`; the largest per folder is 0.181 to 0.299 pp [chk 7]. Experiment 1 therefore reports "coulomb counting vs reference" as a column.
- Reason: positive discharge is the spec's convention. The reference uses the counter because it is the cycler's own coulomb count, and the spec defines the reference SoC as coulomb counting. The baseline uses the logged current because that is all a BMS sees, and the filter sees the same current. S4 states that the reference is itself coulomb counting (spec).
- Evidence: the cycler writes discharge as negative current and a falling counter [inv: *Sign convention*]. In `25degC/551_Cap_1C`, Current goes from 0 to -3.0011 A at Time 60.005 s and Voltage falls from 4.1927 to 4.0928 V; in `25degC/551_Charge2`, Current goes from 0 to +2.9985 A at 3600.005 s and Voltage rises from 3.1220 to 3.4144 V [chk 19]. No Charge file has negative current [inv: *I min / max*]. Drive files open at Ah[0] between -0.00005 and 0 Ah [chk 16]. The integral of Current over Time matches the counter to within 0.01 Ah in every drive file except `602_Mixed4` [inv: flags].

### 1.12 2RC fit rules (S1)

**Per HPPC level the 2RC parameters come from two fits. (a) R1 and tau1 from one least-squares fit over the four discharge pulses D1 to D4 and their 40 s rests (0.1 s data), with tau1 in [0.5, 30] s and R0 held fixed per level at the step-rule value (median over the usable discharge pulses). (b) R2 and tau2 from a fit on the 3600 s rest after the move at that level (10 s data), with the tau1 branch taken from (a) and tau2 in [30, 3600] s. R0 is read as dV/dI over the first sample at which the current reaches at least 98 % of the commanded value, counted from the pulse's first row with current and within 0.3 s. R0 is fixed per level from the step rule (median over the usable discharge pulses) and R1, tau1 are fitted with R0 held fixed. Pulses that never reach 98 % within 0.3 s, and pulses cut by the 2.8 V or 4.2 V limit, are left out and counted in `results/model_fit.md`. This deviates from the spec, which fits R1, tau1, R2 and tau2 once "on the pulse plus its relaxation" with tau2 up to t_rest, and it makes the spec's "first sample" R0 rule precise.**

- Reason: a 40 s rest cannot identify tau2; the spec's bound [30, t_rest] would shrink to [30, 40] s. The 1 h rests are the only rests long enough, so t_rest = 3600 s, and their 10 s logging is a third of tau2's lower bound. Fitting each branch on the data that resolves it, and fixing the tau1 branch in (b), keeps the two branches from swapping, which is what the spec's bounds are for. The R0 rule reads the step only once the commanded current is flowing, because the first sample of some cold pulses is still ramping. R0, R1, tau1 (from (a)) come from the pulses of level k, which start at the counter value where move k-1 ended (level 1: counter 0). R2, tau2 (from (b)) come from the 1 h rest after move k, which lies at the counter value where move k ended, where level k+1's pulses start. Each parameter is tabulated at the SoC of the counter value it was measured at, 1 - Ah_removed / Q_ref, and the export resamples each table onto the 101-point grid (spec, S1). The last level has no 1 h rest (1.2).
- Evidence [chk 10]:
  - Rests are 40.0 s after discharge pulses, 610.0 s after charge pulses and 3600.105 to 3600.131 s after moves, so t_rest = 3600 s. A 1 h rest follows the move of every level except the last (10 / 11 / 10 / 9 / 9 / 8 rests at 40 / 25 / 10 / 0 / -10 / -20 degC). The last move is followed by a 3660 s final rest, except in `25degC/549_HPPC`, which ends there.
  - The first row of a discharge pulse reaches 98 % of the command in all pulses at 40, 25, 10 and 0 degC, in 32 of 40 (80.0 %) at -10 degC and in 29 of 36 (80.6 %) at -20 degC. The 15 exceptions (8 at -10 degC, D1 at levels 1 to 8; 7 at -20 degC, D2 at levels 1 to 7), all at about 1.5 A, reach 98 % on the pulse's third row: 0.194 to 0.205 s after the pulse's first row, but 0.303 to 0.310 s after the last row before it. Counted from the first row with current, every cold discharge pulse passes the 0.3 s test.
  - Discharge pulses that reach 2.8 V (all end before 9.5 s): 3 / 6 / 4 / 5 / 4 / 2 at 40 / 25 / 10 / 0 / -10 / -20 degC. Charge pulses that reach 4.2 V: 5 / 7 / 9 / 6 / 11 / 11. The fit uses discharge pulses only, so the 4.2 V pulses are never in it.
  - Discharge pulses usable for R0 (within 0.3 s and not at 2.8 V): 41 / 42 / 40 / 35 / 36 / 34 of 44 / 48 / 44 / 40 / 40 / 36.
  - The 1 h rests have not fully relaxed at the cold end: relaxation over the last 600 s, median / max, is 0.16 / 2.02 mV at 40 degC, 0.17 / 2.70 at 25, 0.25 / 2.19 at 10, 0.34 / 1.86 at 0, 1.01 / 2.69 at -10 and 2.02 / 5.90 mV at -20 degC (section 3 of this file).

- Fit details that affect results (recorded before the corrected results are produced): fit (a) uses one free constant offset per pulse window, so the slow branch is not modelled inside the 50 s windows; fit (b) uses the rows of the 3600 s rest (10 s logging) with a free asymptote, and derives R2 = B / (I_move (1 - exp(-T_move / tau2))) from the fitted slow amplitude B, assuming V2 = 0 at the start of the move. If the open-loop validation after the OCV refinement of 1.1 still fails the gate, the next recorded step is to carry the slow-branch state through the test instead of assuming V2 = 0.

### 1.15 EKF noise settings (S2, frozen)

**q_soc = 1e-07 1/s, q_v = 1e-06 V^2/s and r = 0.01 V^2, with P0 = diag(0.2^2, 0.05^2, 0.05^2) as in the spec. They are frozen in `results/ekf_config.json` and used unchanged for every experiment, run and temperature. There is no re-tuning on the evaluation set.**

- Reason: they give the lowest objective on the tuning cycle (1.3), 0.7480 pp, among the 288 grid points and the 27 refinement points of `results/tuning.md`; the best grid point alone gives 0.7480 pp. The objective is the mean of the experiment-2 and experiment-4 SoC RMSE, and the q_soc grid is eight decades long (1.16).
- The best grid point is at the edge of the grid in: none. The refined point is at the end of the refinement range in: none. The frozen point is interior to the search in all three parameters: it is not at the edge of the grid or at the end of the refinement range, and its neighbours one decade or a factor of 3 away in each parameter score worse.

### 1.16 S2 refinements after the first full run (recorded before the corrected results are produced)

- **The SoC clamp of Eq. 10 is applied as a covariance-consistent projection, not as a truncation of SoC alone.** When the updated SoC lands outside [0, 1], the whole state is moved along the covariance onto the bound: x_c = x - P e1 (x_SoC - bound) / P_SoC,SoC, so V1 and V2 move together with SoC by their correlation with it; P is left unchanged.
  - Reason: SoC, V1 and V2 are not separately observable from one voltage, so their joint uncertainty stays large along the direction that leaves the voltage unchanged, with SoC and V2 positively correlated. With a plain truncation, an innovation that pushes SoC above 100 % keeps its V2 part, which lowers the next predicted voltage, which enlarges the next innovation: V2 ran away to thousands of volts and the estimate stayed pinned at 100 % while the cell discharged. In the first full run 33 of the 62 evaluation runs exceeded 3 pp RMSE in experiment 1 (`results/ekf_results.csv` as committed in ed7774d; the 25 degC maximum RMSE was 14.70 pp on LA92) while plain coulomb counting stayed within 0.3 pp.
  - Evidence: traced on `0degC/589_US06` with the frozen settings of 1.15: V2 = 2.39 V after 60 s, 185 V after 120 s, 3512 V after 240 s, with the innovation above 2 V and the SoC estimate clamped at 1.0 from 60 s on (manual diagnostic, not reproduced by a script in this repo, so these V2 numbers must not be quoted in the README; the corrected run's experiment-1 table in `results/ekf_summary.md` is the reproducible evidence).
- **The tuning objective becomes the mean of the experiment-2 and experiment-4 SoC RMSE over the evaluation window on the tuning cycle, and the q_soc grid is extended by two decades downward (1e-12 to 1e-5).** 1.15 is re-run and rewritten with the new frozen values; nothing else in the tuning procedure changes.
  - Reason: the experiment-2 objective alone drove q_soc to the lowest grid value and then to the end of the refinement range (1.15), which makes the filter a coulomb counter after its first correction. Such a filter cannot keep a current-sensor offset bounded, which is the first README claim. Adding experiment 4 to the objective rewards the voltage feedback that bounds the offset, on the tuning cycle only. The grid extension records whether the minimum is interior.
  - The spec's rules stand: tuning on 25degC 551_Mixed1 only, one point per decade, one refinement by factors of 3, frozen afterwards, no re-tuning on the evaluation set.

### 1.17 C port rules fixed in S3

- **`dt = 0` is accepted by `ekf_step` and means update only (no predict); only `dt < 0` is an error.** The spec lists "dt <= 0" among the error cases. The Python reference treats the first processed sample with dt = 0 (update only) and the C port mirrors it, so the first row of every run and any repeated-timestamp row goes through the same path in both. Non-finite inputs (NaN or an infinity, in `ekf_step` and in `soc0` of `ekf_init`) are rejected without changing the state; a temperature outside the table range is clamped and flagged with a warning status.
- **The ARM CI job installs the pinned Arm GNU Toolchain 15.3.Rel1 tarball, verified by sha256, not the distribution package.** `results/c_footprint.md` is committed and CI checks it byte for byte, so the compiler that produced it must be the one CI uses; the distribution package is an older GCC.
- **Q_ref reaches the C port through the generated header: `python/export_params.py` emits `EKF_Q_REF_AH` from `Q_REF_AH` in `python/ekfsoc/constants.py`, so the value lives in one place.** (The first S3 build had it as a constant in `c/src/ekf.c`; the simplification pass moved it.)
- Evidence: `results/equivalence.md` (63 runs, experiment 1: float max 1.85e-02 pp against 0.1 pp, double max 5.00e-09 pp against 1e-3 pp), `results/c_footprint.md` (19,344 B of .text after the simplification pass, 0 B .data and .bss, sizeof(ekf_t) = 60 B, only `expf` undefined).

### 1.18 Capacity-error sensitivity (S5)

- **The filter and the coulomb counter are run with the capacity set to 0.95 Q_ref and 1.05 Q_ref; the reference SoC, the evaluation window (C14) and the frozen noise settings (1.15) do not change.** Experiment 1 (correct initial SoC) on the 63 runs of 1.5. The nominal column is the experiment-1 row of `results/ekf_results.csv`, not a rerun. Q_ref enters the filter through the SoC row of Eq. 6 only, so a keyword argument of `run_ekf` carries the scaled value; the C port is untouched (its `EKF_Q_REF_AH` is a compile-time constant).
- Reason: a wrong capacity is what ageing looks like to a coulomb counter: its error grows with the charge throughput, about 5 % of the SoC swing at 5 % capacity error. The EKF corrects it through the voltage. The 5 % of the spec brackets the measured fade of `results/capacity.md`. The truth does not move because the reference SoC is the cycler's counter against the measured capacity (1.10).
- Report: `results/sensitivity.md`, per temperature the mean SoC RMSE over the folder's runs at -5 %, nominal and +5 %, for the EKF and for coulomb counting, with the largest per-run RMSE named; `results/sensitivity.csv` holds every run. Plot `results/plots/s5/sensitivity_capacity.png`: RMSE against temperature, one line per capacity scaling, EKF solid and coulomb counting dashed.

### 1.19 Sample-rate sensitivity: the 1 Hz decimated run (S5)

- **Decimation keeps every 10th kept row of the loader's run (rows 0, 10, 20, ...), recomputes dt between the kept rows and changes nothing else: the kept sample's current is held over the whole step (C11), the reference SoC is read at the kept rows from the counter, the frozen noise settings apply unchanged (the process noise scales with dt, 1.15) and the window is C14 on the kept rows.** Experiment 1 on the 63 runs of 1.5; coulomb counting at 1 Hz on the same rows is the comparator; the 0.1 s values come from `results/ekf_results.csv`.
- Reason: a 1 Hz logger without current averaging records exactly these rows; averaging the ten currents would model an integrating sensor, a different check. Keeping the hold rule makes the decimated run the same filter on sparser data, so the difference measures the sample rate alone. The coulomb-counting comparator separates the hold error of the input, which both estimators share, from the filter's own loss.
- Report: `results/sensitivity.md`, per temperature the mean SoC RMSE and the largest max |error| at 0.1 s and at 1 s for the EKF and for coulomb counting; plot `results/plots/s5/sensitivity_rate.png`.

### 1.20 Octave port rules (S5)

- **`octave/` holds the filter only (the Eq. 4 lookups, Eq. 6 to 11 with the projection limit of 1.16), function for function after `python/ekfsoc/ekf.py` and `model.py` with the same operation order, in double precision, in plain Octave with no packages and no syntax newer than Octave 8 (Ubuntu's package), so the same files run under the GNU Octave 8.4.0 used locally (the official `gnuoctave/octave:8.4.0` container image) and the distribution Octave in CI.** The loader, the fit and the metrics are not ported: the port reads `results/params.csv` and the input CSV of `c/tools/ekf_run_csv` (dt_s, i_a, v_v, t_degc) with the same command line (input, output, q_soc, q_v, r, soc0), P0 as in 1.15, and writes the SoC with 17 significant digits.

Its `--fixtures` mode runs the three fixtures of `c/tests/fixtures/` against their `soc_expected`, and CI runs that mode in a fourth job with the distribution Octave.
- Reason: the spec asks for an `.m` EKF that reproduces the Python results; the C port already fixed the input format, the command line and the equivalence procedure, so the Octave port reuses them instead of defining a second convention. 17 digits (not the 10 of the C tool) because Octave's double arithmetic can be compared at full resolution; differences of an ulp in `exp` or in the matrix products may remain and are far below the threshold.
- Plot: `results/plots/s5/equivalence_octave_25degC.png`, Python minus Octave SoC in pp over time on US06 at 25 degC.

### 1.21 3RC model and fit rules (S6)

**S6 adds a third RC branch, between the fast and the slow one, as a second model beside the 2RC model of v1.0, which stays as it is. The new branch is numbered 3 (R3, tau3, V3), so branches 1 and 2 keep their v1.0 meaning and the 2RC state [SoC, V1, V2] is the first three entries of the 3RC state [SoC, V1, V2, V3]. OCV, dOCV/dSoC and R0 are the v1.0 tables, unchanged; R1, tau1, R2 and tau2 are refitted together with R3 and tau3.**

- Fit (a), 3RC: R1, tau1, R3 and tau3 from one least-squares fit over the four discharge pulses D1 to D4 and their 40 s rests (0.1 s data), with R0 held fixed per level at its v1.0 value and one free constant offset per pulse window, as in 1.12. Bounds: tau1 in [0.5, 10] s, tau3 in [10, 50] s.
- Fit (b), 3RC: R2 and tau2 from the 3600 s rest after the move, as in 1.12, with the tau1 and tau3 branches of (a) added back over the move and the rest, and tau2 in [50, 3600] s. V2 = 0 at the start of the move, as in 1.12.
- Export: the level fits go to `results/fit_levels_3rc.csv` and are resampled onto the 101-point grid as in 1.12 into `results/params_3rc.csv`, which holds OCV, dOCV/dSoC and R0 as read from `results/params.csv` and the six refitted RC tables. `results/model_fit_3rc.md` gives, per temperature, the failed fits, the median tau3 and R3, and the number of levels whose tau1, tau3 or tau2 ends on a bound (within 0.1 % of an end).
- Reason: the v1.0 level fits have tau1 from 0.53 to 6.2 s and tau2 from 99.6 to 2128 s (`results/fit_levels.csv`), so no branch follows the voltage between about 10 s and 100 s. The upper bound of tau3 is the length of one pulse window, 50 s: a longer time constant cannot be told apart from the window's free offset. The splits at 10 s (tau1 and tau3) and at 50 s (tau3 and tau2) keep the three branches from swapping, as the bounds of 1.12 do for two, and no v1.0 level fit lies outside the new tau1 or tau2 bound. The 610 s rests after the charge pulses would resolve tau3 better, but the fits use discharge pulses only (1.12), and adding charge pulses would change two things at once.
- Risk, recorded before the fit: tau3 is identified from 50 s windows with a free offset, so it may end on a bound at many levels. The count is reported in `results/model_fit_3rc.md`, and 1.22 judges the model by its results, not by its fit.
- Validation: the open-loop US06 run of 1.4 for the 3RC model at every temperature, next to the 2RC values, in `results/model_fit_3rc.md`. The S1 sanity gate (30 mV at 25 degC) applies to the 3RC model as well.

### 1.22 3RC evaluation and adoption rule (S6)

**The 3RC filter runs experiments 1 to 4 on the 63 runs of 1.5 with the frozen noise settings of 1.15 unchanged: q_v applies to V3 as to V1 and V2, and P0 gets p0_v_std for V3. There is no re-tuning. The 3RC model is adopted (the README recommends it, and S6 ports it to C under 1.23) if and only if its experiment-1 mean SoC RMSE over the evaluation runs (tuning cycle excluded) is lower than the 2RC value at four or more of the six temperatures and higher at none by more than 0.10 pp. Otherwise S6 reports a negative result and the C port is not touched.**

- Reason: fixed noise settings make the comparison measure the model change alone; a re-tune would add a second change, judged on the tuning cycle only (1.3). Experiment 1 is the criterion because there the model, not the recovery from a wrong start, decides the error, and it is the gate metric of S2. Four of six temperatures with none worse by more than 0.10 pp asks for a broad improvement, not a win at one temperature paid for at another; 0.10 pp is about a tenth of the 25 degC mean of 0.87 pp (`results/ekf_summary.md`). The rule is fixed here, before any 3RC result exists, and either outcome is published.
- Report: `results/ekf_results_3rc.csv` (the columns of `results/ekf_results.csv`) and `results/model_compare.md`: per temperature the open-loop RMSE, the experiment-1 and experiment-4 mean SoC RMSE and the reached counts of experiments 2 and 3, 2RC next to 3RC, with the adoption verdict computed by the script. No v1.0 or v1.1 result file is rewritten.

### 1.23 C port of the 3RC filter (S6, only if 1.22 adopts the model)

### 1.24 Outcome of the 3RC fit of 1.21 (S6, recorded before any revision)

**Under the rules of 1.21 the 3RC model fails the S1 sanity gate: its open-loop US06 RMSE is 46.79 mV at 25 degC against 29.79 mV for the 2RC model, and it is higher than the 2RC value at five of the six temperatures (`results/model_fit_3rc.md`). It is not adopted, and the experiments of 1.22 and the C port of 1.23 are not run on it. This is a negative result of the pre-registered procedure, published as such; the code was checked and is not the cause.**

- What the fit does: on the data it is fitted to, the middle branch helps. At 25 degC level 5 the pulse-window RMSE of fit (a) falls from 4.44 mV (2RC) to 2.34 mV (3RC), with tau1 moving from 4.49 s to 1.30 s and the middle branch taking R3 = 11.94 mOhm at tau3 = 29.3 s (`results/fit_levels.csv`, `results/fit_levels_3rc.csv`).
- Why it fails on US06: R1 + R3 + R2 exceeds the 2RC R1 + R2 at most levels (25 degC level 5: 28.1 against 19.8 mOhm), so the model drops too much voltage under sustained load. Two causes, both measured:
  - Fit (a) gives the middle branch part of the slow branch's change inside each 50 s window, which the window's free offset cannot absorb: on synthetic pulses with a 5 mOhm, 400 s slow branch it returns R3 and tau3 about 11 % and 24 % too high, and the true values without it (`python/tests/test_fit.py`, the 3RC fast-fit test). Fit (b) then lowers R2 only a little.
  - The US06 data asks for little middle-branch resistance at all. With the 2RC tables unchanged, the best constant third branch on a grid of R3 in {2, 5, 10, 20} mOhm and tau3 in {10, 20, 30, 50} s, chosen on the US06 file itself, lowers the open-loop RMSE by only 0.75 to 2.48 mV, with R3 of 2 to 10 mOhm (`results/model_fit_3rc.md`, ceiling section). The 3RC fit's median R3 is 7.68 to 29.79 mOhm. At 40, 25 and 10 degC the best grid value is the smallest one, 2 mOhm.
- Code check: the tables of `results/params_3rc.csv` were rebuilt from the level fits and match to the 6-digit rounding; an independent three-branch loop reproduces the 46.79 mV; the two fits recover known R1, tau1, R3, tau3 and R2, tau2 from synthetic data; and the 2RC path stays byte-identical. Swapping branch sets on US06 at 25 degC puts the extra error in the fast branches of fit (a), not in fit (b) or the simulation.
- What this means for the motivation of S6: a third branch can improve the US06 voltage by at most about 1 to 2.5 mV (2 to 9 %) while the other branches stay as fitted. The larger gain an exploratory regression suggested before S6 came mostly from the free constant offset of that regression, not from the branch.
- Any revised fit is a post-hoc change: it is recorded as its own decision before it runs, the gate and the adoption rule of 1.22 stay as they are, and this entry and `results/model_fit_3rc.md` stay as the record of the procedure fixed in advance.

### 1.26 S6: plan for a second 3RC fit, written before it runs

**A second 3RC model is fitted, the post-hoc revision that 1.24 and 1.25 allow. Every 2RC table of `results/params.csv` stays as it is; only R3 and tau3 are fitted, per HPPC level, on the whole pulse train of that level with the other branches simulated from their tables. The model is judged by the S1 gate and the adoption rule of 1.22, both unchanged. The 3RC model of 1.21 and its files stay as the record of the procedure fixed in advance.**

- Reason: 1.24 found two causes for the failure of 1.21, and this fit removes both. (1) Refitting R1, tau1, R2 and tau2 together with the new branch let it take part of the slow branch's change; here those four tables are not refitted, which is also the assumption under which 1.25 bounds the gain. (2) The 50 s windows of fit (a), each with its own free offset, cannot resolve a 20 to 50 s time constant; here the cost includes the four 610 s rests after the charge pulses, which 1.21 named as the data that would resolve tau3 better and left out to change one thing at a time.
- Data, per level: the rows from the first row of D1 to the last row before the move, that is the pulses D1, C1, D2, C2, D3, C3, D4, C4 in running order with the 40 s rest after each discharge pulse and the 610 s rest after each charge pulse (600 s after C4). The pulses and the 40 s rests are logged at 0.1 s. Each 610 s rest is logged at 0.1 s for its first 10 s, 1 s up to 60 s, 10 s up to 600 s and 0.1 s for its last 10 s, except the rest after C4, which has no 0.1 s tail. A level with a discharge pulse cut at 2.8 V is not fitted and counts as failed, as 1.12 leaves such pulses out of fit (a): near the cutoff the model's error steps from one charge rest to the next, which neither the branch nor a single offset can follow. Charge pulses cut at 4.2 V stay in, with their current as measured. The last level has a pulse train but no 1 h rest; it is fitted like the others unless a pulse is cut at 2.8 V.
- Model: the 2RC tables at the reference SoC and the measured cell temperature, plus one branch with R3 and tau3 constant over the level, simulated through the whole train by Eq. 1 and Eq. 2 with the measured current. The simulation starts at the first row of the previous level's move with every branch voltage 0, the state 1.12 assumes there, and runs through the move and its 1 h rest into the train, so the slow branch the move leaves behind decays as the tables say. Level 1 starts at the first row of the file, 10 s before D1. Only the rows of the train enter the cost.
- Cost: the measured minus model voltage on the rest rows only, from 20 s after the last row of each pulse to the end of its rest. Rest rows carry no current, so R0 does not enter the cost. The R1, tau1, R2 and tau2 tables do, through the branch voltages the pulses leave behind. 20 s is more than three times the longest tau1 of the v1.0 fits, 6.2 s, so the fast branch is below 4 % of its value at the end of the pulse. Every cost row counts once, whatever its logging step, as in fit (a) of 1.12; the rows 20 to 40 s after the discharge pulses and the 0.1 s tails of the charge rests therefore carry most of the weight.
- Free parameters, three per level: R3 in [0, 0.5] Ohm, tau3 in [10, 100] s and one constant offset for the whole train, started at 10 mOhm, 30 s and 0 mV and solved with the bounded least squares of 1.12. The single offset absorbs a level-wide bias of the OCV table, such as the unrelaxed cold rests of 1.12. It cannot imitate a 20 to 100 s decay that repeats in four 610 s rests, unlike the offset per 50 s window of 1.21. The tau3 bound starts at 10 s, where 1.21 splits tau1 from tau3. It ends at 100 s, the lower end of the v1.0 tau2 fits (99.6 s), so the new branch cannot become a second slow branch, except at the five levels at 40 and 25 degC where tau2 itself is 99.6 to 147 s (`results/fit_levels.csv`); the report lists those levels.
- Export: the level fits go to `results/fit_levels_3rc_rest.csv`, with fit_ok, the rest-row RMSE and the offset per level, and a level left out for a pulse cut at 2.8 V marked as such. R3 and tau3 are tabulated at the SoC of the level's first pulse row and resampled onto the 101-point grid by the rule of 1.12 into `results/params_3rc_rest.csv`, which holds every table of `results/params.csv` unchanged plus R3 and tau3. A level whose fit fails gives no value and is skipped, as in 1.12.
- Report: `python/fit_3rc_rest.py` (`make fit-3rc-rest`) writes `results/model_fit_3rc_rest.md` with, per temperature, the failed fits and the levels left out for a cut pulse, the levels whose tau3 ends on a bound (within 0.1 % of an end, as in 1.21), the median R3, tau3 and offset, the open-loop US06 RMSE next to the 2RC value with the S1 gate (30 mV at 25 degC), and the pooled open-loop RMSE over every drive run, scored as in 1.25 and printed next to the ceiling of 1.25. `results/plots/s6/params_3rc_rest.png` draws R3 and tau3 against SoC (1.14).
- Evaluation: if the gate passes, 1.22 runs on this model unchanged: experiments 1 to 4 on the 63 runs with the frozen noise settings, q_v and p0_v_std for V3 as for V1 and V2, the adoption rule of four or more of six temperatures lower and none higher by more than 0.10 pp, and the report files that 1.22 names, which the 1.21 model never reached. If the model is adopted, the C build of 1.23 takes its tables from `results/params_3rc_rest.csv`. If the gate or the adoption rule fails, S6 reports a second negative result, and no further revision of the middle branch runs under S6.
- Risks, recorded before the fit: the R2 and tau2 tables come from the 1 h rests and may not hold on the 610 s rests, and the cost then pushes the difference into R3. tau3 may end on its upper bound at levels where that happens; the count is reported. 1.25 shows that the drive cycles ask for R3 of 5 to 35 mOhm at tau3 of 20 to 30 s, but that table was chosen on the drive cycles, and this fit never sees them.

### 1.28 S7: R1 that depends on current (model and fit)

**The 2RC-I model of S7 is the 2RC model of v1.0 with one change: R1 is a table over temperature, SoC and the magnitude of the current, read at the current held over each step (`docs/model.md` Eq. 1, Eq. 4b, Eq. 6). OCV, dOCV/dSoC, R0, tau1, R2 and tau2 are the tables of `results/params.csv`, unchanged. R1 is fitted once per usable discharge pulse with fit (a) of 1.12, with R0 and tau1 held at the level's values from fit (a). There is one fit; no revision of it runs under S7.**

- Reason: over the usable HPPC discharge pulses, the voltage drop after 10 s per ampere falls from the smallest to the largest pulse current at every temperature, little at 40 degC and most at -20 degC. The fit holds R0 at the level's median step-rule value, so R1_w carries this fall of the total. The drop minus each pulse's own step-rule R0 is not used as the measure: the step-rule R0 of D1 lies well below that of D2 at 40 to 10 degC, so subtracting it would move that difference into R1, and at -10 degC the ramping D1 pulses of 1.12 make that measure not fall at all. R0 stays a table over (T, SoC). This was measured before the stage and is exploratory; the S7 report publishes the fitted values. One axis on one table keeps the filter's structure: the current is an input, so reading R1 at it changes only the R1 entry of B (Eq. 6), not the state, F, H or the noise settings.
- Fit, per level and per usable discharge pulse w (usable as in 1.12: 98 % of the command within 0.3 s, not cut at 2.8 V): the window of fit (a), the pulse and its 40 s rest, with z_w = V - OCV(SoC_ref) + R0 · I. R0 and tau1 are the level's values as fit (a) (`fit_level_fast`) computes them, not read back from the rounded `results/fit_levels.csv`. V1 follows Eq. 1 with R1_w, that tau1 and V1 = 0 at the first pulse row, and one free constant per window. With tau1 fixed the window is linear in R1_w and its constant, so R1_w is the least-squares solution of one line, with no iteration and no start value. Its current is the pulse's median current (i_cmd of `hppc.py`). A pulse with R1_w at or below 0 is reported and left out. A level whose fit (a) fails has no R0 or tau1 and gives no pulse.
- Table: the current axis has the nodes 0.75, 1.5, 3, 6, 12 and 18 A, the discharge pulse currents of the campaign; the 10 degC pulses of test 575 (2.9 to 17.4 A) lie between nodes. Per level, the R1_w are interpolated linearly in current onto the nodes and held at the end values beyond the level's smallest and largest usable pulse current. At the low-SoC levels where D4, or D3 and D4, are cut at 2.8 V, the 12 and 18 A nodes therefore take the 6 or 12 A value. Per current node, the level values are resampled over SoC by the rule of 1.12. The result goes to `results/r1_current.csv` (T_degC, I_A, soc, R1_ohm), and every pulse fit to `results/fit_pulses_r1i.csv`.
- Approximations, recorded before the fit:
  - tau1 stays fixed. With a fixed capacitance, tau1 would fall with R1; fixing tau1 keeps the window linear and the change to one table.
  - R2 and tau2 were fitted in v1.0 with the v1.0 R1 added back over the move at 0.9 A, and are not refitted.
  - Charge and regen use the discharge values at |I|, as in 1.12.
  - Pulse current and position are the same variable: the smallest current is always D1, the only pulse that follows the move and its 1 h rest, while D2 to D4 follow a charge pulse and a 610 s rest. A history effect on D1 cannot be told apart from a current effect, and the D1 value sets R1 for every |I| below it. The report gives the D1 values in their own column and says so.
  - Below the smallest pulse current R1 is held at that pulse's value: 3 A at 40, 25 and 0 degC, 2.9 A at 10 degC, 1.5 A at -10 degC and 0.75 A at -20 degC. Drive cycles run often below 3 A, and if R1 keeps rising toward low current, the model still under-predicts the drop there at the warm temperatures.
- Report: `python/fit_r1_current.py` (`make fit-r1i`) writes `results/model_fit_r1i.md`. Per temperature and pulse number (D1 to D4, so D1 has its own column) it gives the median pulse current and R1 next to the v1.0 R1 median, and the pulses left out. It also gives the open-loop US06 RMSE next to the 2RC value with the S1 gate (30 mV at 25 degC), and the open-loop RMSE pooled over every drive run, scored as in 1.25. `results/plots/s7/r1_current.png` draws R1 against current (1.14). If the gate fails, S7 reports a negative result, and 1.29 and 1.30 do not run.

### 1.32 S8: R1 by the sign of the current, the stage plan and its adoption rule

**S8 asks why the 2RC-I model makes US06 worse from 10 degC down (1.31), and tests the sign of the current first. B is written only after A's outcome is recorded and only if A meets the test of 1.33; C after B's outcome, or after A's if B does not run; D last. - Reason: on the open-loop US06 runs the 2RC-I model moves the mean error (model minus measured) toward a model voltage that is too high under regen and too low under discharge between 1.5 and 6 A, from 0 degC down; at -20 degC the regen mean goes from +41.8 to +73.1 mV (manual diagnostic of 2026-11-02, not reproduced by a script in this repo, so these numbers must not be quoted in the README; step A's report reproduces the band split). Both directions mean more polarisation than the cell shows. Under regen, 1.28 reads the discharge R1 at |I|, and US06 regen currents are small against its discharge peaks (at most 6.0 A at 40 to 10 degC, 3.1 A at 0 and -10 degC, 1.6 A at -20 degC), so regen reads the low-current end of the table: at 1.5 A the median of the 2RC-I R1 over the grid points SoC 0.5 to 0.95, divided by the median of the v1.0 R1 over the same points, is 1.3 at 25 degC, 1.4 at 0 degC, 2.0 at -10 degC and 1.7 at -20 degC (same diagnostic; the median of the pointwise ratios gives 1.3, 1.5, 2.0 and 1.8). The band errors include the branch states carried from the rows before, so they support the hypothesis without proving that the R1 read under regen causes the bias. The ablation of A tests it with no new fit; a fitted charge-side table (B) runs only if A meets the test of 1.33, which is separate from adoption.
- The evaluation set is not fresh. The 62 runs of 1.5 have been inspected in S2, S5, S6 and S7, and the S8 hypothesis comes from the S7 results and traces of these same runs. The dataset has no unseen drive run to hold out, so S8 results are a comparison on a repeatedly inspected set, not a confirmation, and the README says so where it reports them.
- Noise settings: steps A to C change the model only, and every run uses the frozen noise settings of 1.15. Step D changes the noise settings by design, so the freeze does not bind it; D's entry fixes, before it runs, the covariance policy, its bounds, and which runs any tuning may use.
- Adoption rule for every S8 model change, fixed here before any S8 result exists. The candidate is compared with the incumbent, the model adopted at that point (2RC-I for step A). The candidate is adopted if and only if all of these hold:
  1. the S1 gate of 1.28 (open-loop US06 RMSE at most 30 mV at 25 degC);
  2. the rule of 1.22 against the incumbent: experiment-1 mean SoC RMSE lower at four or more of the six temperatures and higher at none by more than 0.10 pp;
  3. no evaluation run's experiment-1 SoC RMSE is higher than the incumbent's by more than 0.50 pp;
  4. no drive cycle's experiment-1 mean SoC RMSE over its evaluation runs (the twelve cycles of `results/ekf_results.csv`) is higher than the incumbent's by more than 0.25 pp;
  5. the largest experiment-1 SoC RMSE over the evaluation runs is not higher than the incumbent's;
  6. the experiment-4 mean SoC RMSE is higher at no temperature by more than 0.10 pp, and the reached counts of experiments 2 and 3 are lower at no temperature.
- Guards 3 to 5 hold against the incumbent and also against 2RC-I, the model S8 starts from, so the limits are a budget for the whole stage: steps that each stay inside them cannot add up to a larger loss. For step A the two comparisons are the same. Every guard is evaluated on unrounded values; the report rounds only for display.
- Reason for the guards: the rule of 1.22 alone adopted a model that doubled the US06 error in the cold (1.31). Guards 3 to 5 bound the loss on a single run, a single cycle and the worst case, and guard 6 keeps the recovery from a wrong start and the offset bound of 1.16. The limits are set knowing the S7 changes: under them, S7 would have failed guard 3 on six runs (US06 at 10, 0, -10 and -20 degC; Mix 6 at -10 and -20 degC), guard 4 on US06 (+1.42 pp) and Mix 6 (+0.61 pp), and guard 6 on the -20 degC reached counts of experiment 2 and the high start of experiment 3. Its -20 degC experiment-4 change, +0.0998 pp, stays inside the 0.10 pp limit.
- Each S8 report also gives the candidate against the 2RC model of v1.0, per temperature and for US06, so the reader sees where S8 stands against the filter of v1.0 as well as against the incumbent.

### 1.35 S8 step B: a charge-side R1 fitted on the HPPC charge pulses (the "2RC-Ic" model)

**The 2RC-Ic model is the 2RC-I model of 1.28 with one change: when the current held over the step is negative, R1 is read with Eq. 4b from a charge-side table over (T, SoC, |I|), `results/r1_charge.csv`; otherwise from the 2RC-I table, as before. The charge-side table is fitted once per HPPC charge pulse not cut by the 4.2 V limit, with the one-line fit of 1.28 and R0 and tau1 held at the level's discharge values, after removing the slow-branch decay carried into the window. Every other table and the noise settings of 1.15 are unchanged. Adoption follows 1.32 with 2RC-I as the incumbent. There is one fit; no revision of it runs under S8.**

- Reason: step A (1.34) showed that the R1 read under regen carries most of the US06 regression of 2RC-I, but that the v1.0 R1 as the regen value costs other runs. The HPPC test has four charge pulses per level, C1 to C4, at currents that cover the regen of the drive cycles (1.5 / 3 / 4.5 / 6 A at 40 and 25 degC down to 0.37 / 0.75 / 1.12 / 1.5 A at -20 degC, 1.2; US06 regen reaches 6.0, 3.1 and 1.6 A at 40 to 10, 0 and -10, and -20 degC, 1.32). A measured charge value replaces a borrowed one.
- Measured before the stage (exploratory, manual diagnostics of 2026-11-03 by the author and by one reviewer, not reproduced by a script in this repo, so these numbers must not be quoted in the README; the step B report publishes the fitted values):
  - Charge pulses: 44, 48, 44, 40, 40 and 36 at 40, 25, 10, 0, -10 and -20 degC, of which 5 / 7 / 9 / 6 / 11 / 11 reach 4.2 V (1.12). A further 0 / 1 / 0 / 4 / 4 / 9 miss the 0.3 s rule of 1.12 by reaching 98 % of the command 0.301 to 0.305 s after their first row; they are kept (below).
  - Within a level, the charge R1 falls with current, little when warm and much in the cold (about 25 to 9 mOhm from C1 to C4 at mid SoC at -10 degC). At the low-SoC levels at 0 and -20 degC, C4 lies above C3. Medians over pulse numbers mix SoC, because the usable pulses differ by level, and are not used here.
  - The fitted charge R1 absorbs any difference between the charge and the discharge R0 almost one to one (a synthetic window gives dR1/dR0 = +0.99). The charge step-rule R0 minus the level's discharge R0 has a median of -12 to +5 mOhm per pulse number at 0 degC and +16 to +58 mOhm at -20 degC, so at -20 degC much of the charge R1 may be an R0 difference. The model reads the same R0 table under regen, so the sum R0 + R1 at the 10 s scale is what the fit gets right; the split between them, and so the lag, may not be.
- Fit, per level with a successful fit (a) and per charge pulse w not cut by the 4.2 V limit and not shorter than 9 s:
  - Pulses that miss the 0.3 s rule are kept: that rule exists to read the step-rule R0, and this fit reads no R0 from a charge pulse; z_w uses the measured current row by row. Leaving them out would drop the C1 pulses at the cold mid-SoC levels and the -20 degC level at SoC 0.89, where US06 at -20 degC regenerates first.
  - Window: the pulse rows and the rows of its rest with t - t(first rest row) <= 40 s, every logged row, unweighted, as in 1.28. The rest is logged at 0.1 s for about 9 s and then at about 1 s, so a charge window has about 130 rows against about 400 in a discharge window, and the pulse weighs more. The report also gives the fit with rows weighted by dt, as a sensitivity; it does not enter the table. (Measured before the stage: weighting by dt lowers the median charge R1 per pulse number by 1 to 23 %, a single pulse by up to 53 %, more at C3 and C4 than at C1.)
  - z_w = V - OCV(SoC_ref) + R0 · I, with R0 and tau1 the level's values as fit (a) (`fit_level_fast`) computes them, as in 1.28.
  - Carried-in polarisation: each charge pulse follows a discharge pulse by a 40 s rest. The 2RC-I model (its tables, R1 read at the held current) is simulated open loop from a zero state at the level's first pulse row, which follows the 1 h rest after the move, up to the window's first row; from that row on its current is set to zero, and the resulting V1 + V2, the free decay of the state carried in, is added to z_w. The free constant of the window stays, as in fit (a). With tau1 of a few seconds, V1 from the discharge pulse has decayed after 40 s, so the carried term is in practice V2 of fit (b), with the errors of R2 and tau2 that 1.24 to 1.27 record. Measured before the stage with the v1.0 tables: the decay moves over a window by a median of 0.1 to 2.2 mV per temperature and pulse number (0.015 to 6.9 mV over single pulses), against a median R1 response of 8 to 84 mV (6.6 to 123 mV); it raises a pulse's R1 by at most 19.3 % of its uncorrected value (0 degC) and by at most 11.9 % at the other temperatures. The 2RC-I and v1.0 tables give the same R1 to better than 5e-5 relative, and starting the simulation at the previous move changes a pulse's R1 by at most 1.1 %.
  - R1_w is the least-squares slope of one line with tau1 held (`window_r1` of 1.28); its current is the pulse's |i_cmd|, its SoC the level's soc_pulses (the first pulse row, D1), as in 1.28. A pulse with R1_w at or below 0 is reported and left out. A level whose fit (a) fails gives no pulse.
- Table: the charge current axis has the nodes 0.375, 0.75, 1.5, 3 and 6 A, which span the charge pulse currents; 1.12, 2.25 and 4.5 A pulses lie between nodes. Per level, the R1_w are interpolated linearly in current onto the nodes and held beyond the level's smallest and largest pulse in the fit: a level with C1 alone (at high SoC, where C2 to C4 reach 4.2 V) sets every node from its C1 value, and a level without C1 holds its smallest pulse's value down to 0.375 A. Per node, the level values are resampled over SoC by the rule of 1.12, held flat beyond the first and last level with a pulse in the fit. The result goes to `results/r1_charge.csv` (the columns of `results/r1_current.csv`), every pulse fit to `results/fit_pulses_r1c.csv`. Beyond its nodes Eq. 4b clamps the current, as for the discharge table.
- Approximations, recorded before the fit:
  - R0 is the discharge value of fit (a); the charge R1 takes up the R0 difference (above). tau1 is the discharge value; measured before the stage on synthetic windows, a true tau1 1.5 times the held one biases R1 by -3 to -15 %, and one 1.5 times smaller by +1 to +8 %.
  - Inside the window the slow branch's response to the charge pulse itself is left to the free constant, as in fit (a); only the state carried in from before the window is removed.
  - The simulation of the carried-in state reads the 2RC-I tables for the earlier charge pulses too (the charge-side table does not exist yet); each lies at least 610 s before the window.
  - Pulse current and position are the same variable, as for D1 to D4 (1.28): C_n always follows D_n by 40 s, and C1 follows D1, the only pulse after the 1 h rest. The carried-in correction removes the modelled part of that history only.
- Report: `python/fit_r1_charge.py` (`make fit-r1c`) writes `results/model_fit_r1c.md`:
  - per temperature and pulse number (C1 to C4), over the pulses in the fit: the median current, the median R1 with and without the carried-in correction and with rows weighted by dt, the median charge step-rule R0 minus the level's discharge R0, and, for the same pulses, the median v1.0 R1 and the median 2RC-I discharge R1 read from `results/r1_current.csv` at the pulse's current and the level's soc_pulses; and the same comparison as R0 + R1, so a charge-discharge difference is not read as R1 alone;
  - the pulses left out by reason, and the SoC span of the levels with a pulse in the fit;
  - the open-loop US06 RMSE of 2RC-Ic with the S1 gate (guard 1 of 1.32). If the gate fails, step B reports a negative result and the evaluation does not run.
  `results/plots/s8/r1_charge.png` draws the charge R1 against current per temperature next to the 2RC-I discharge R1 (1.14).
- Evaluation: `python/compare_r1c.py` (`make ekf-r1c`) runs experiments 1 to 4 on the 63 runs with the 2RC-Ic tables and writes `results/ekf_results_r1c.csv` and `results/model_compare_r1c.md`, with the sections of `results/model_compare_r1d.md` for 2RC, 2RC-I, 2RC-Id and 2RC-Ic, every guard of 1.32 against 2RC-I (the incumbent and the start of the stage budget are the same model here), and the verdict. `results/plots/s8/model_compare_r1c.png` draws the open-loop and experiment-1 columns. Traces go to `results/runs/r1c/`.
- Rounding, amending 1.32 for steps B to D: the guards are evaluated on the values of the results CSVs (4 decimals). Where a guard's value lies within 1e-4 of its limit (or, for a comparison of two values, the two lie within 1e-4 of each other), the script recomputes it from the unrounded traces, and the unrounded value decides. The report says which guards were recomputed.
- `docs/model.md` notes under Eq. 1, Eq. 4b and Eq. 6 state the 2RC-Ic read (the charge table, its nodes, the clamp) before any result.
- Byte identity: with the step B code every earlier result and trace, the S8 step A ones included, regenerates byte for byte.

### 1.37 S8 step C: R1 at and below the smallest discharge pulse, scaled on the PausCycl files (the "2RC-Il" model)

**The 2RC-Il model is the 2RC-I model of 1.28 with one change: its R1 table over (T, SoC, |I|), `results/r1_low.csv`, is the 2RC-I table with the values at and below each level's smallest pulse current multiplied by one factor r_T per temperature, ramping back to the 2RC-I values at the level's second pulse. There is one fit; no revision of it runs under S8.**

- Reason: 1.28 holds R1 flat below the smallest discharge pulse current (3 A at 40, 25 and 0 degC, 2.9 A at 10 degC, 1.5 A at -10 degC, 0.75 A at -20 degC), where drive cycles spend much of their time, and its smallest pulse, D1, is also the only pulse after the 1 h rest, so its R1 mixes a current and a history effect. The PausCycl files (one per folder, excluded from the evaluation set in 1.5) are drive-like, logged at 0.1 s, and discharge below D1 for much of their length, so they can.
- Measured before the stage (exploratory, manual diagnostics of 2026-11-05, not reproduced by a script in this repo, so these numbers must not be quoted in the README; the step C report publishes the fitted values):
  - PausCycl content: 10 degC 2783 s, 0 degC 2630 s, -10 degC 2215 s, -20 degC 1661 s, each from a full charge (rest voltage 4.18 to 4.19 V) down to SoC about 0.87 to 0.91, with 55 / 54 / 39 / 24 % of rows at a discharge current below D1 (0 < I < I_1) and 17 / 17 / 16 / 14 % regenerating.   - Two shapes were fitted, one temperature at a time (not jointly as below), each with one free offset. A ramp from r · R1(D1) at 0 A up to R1(D1) at D1 gave r = 0.83 / 1.01 / 0.82 / 0.62 at 10 / 0 / -10 / -20 degC and lowered the PausCycl RMSE by less than 0.3 mV (1 %). Scaling R1 by r at and below D1, ramping back to 1 at D2, gave r = 0.92 / 0.98 / 0.86 / 0.78 and lowered it by 0.1 / 0.0 / 1.2 / 2.1 mV (0 to 7 %). - Shape, per level of the 2RC-I fit: s(I) = r_T for I <= I_1, s(I) = r_T + (1 - r_T)(I - I_1) / (I_2 - I_1) for I_1 < I < I_2, and s(I) = 1 for I >= I_2, where I_1 and I_2 are the level's smallest and second-smallest pulse currents in the 2RC-I fit (`results/fit_pulses_r1i.csv`). A level with one pulse in the fit is scaled by r_T at every current; of the two such levels, 25 degC level 12 has r = 1, so only 0 degC level 10 (SoC 0.137) changes, and through the SoC hold of 1.12 its 12 and 18 A values are scaled at 0 degC below that SoC too. The level values and I_1, I_2 come from the unrounded pulse fits, recomputed as `fit_r1_current.py` computes them (I from the unrounded i_cmd); the fit simulates the unrounded table, and the report reads `results/r1_low.csv` back, as 1.28 does. - Fit: the four factors r_10, r_0, r_-10 and r_-20 are fitted jointly by linear least squares on the four PausCycl files (`590_PausCycl_0degC`, `571_PausCycl_10degC`, `604_PausCycl_n10degC`, `611_PausCycl_n20degC`), every row, unweighted, with one free offset per file. The open-loop model (`simulate_open_loop`) runs with the reference SoC from 1.0 and the measured cell temperature, which lies between two grid temperatures for all or nearly all rows of each file (the 10 degC file at 8.6 to 9.9 degC, the 0 degC file at -0.6 to 0.2), so every file depends on two factors; the cross terms are small (in the review, cross-regressor RMS 1.1 to 4.8 mV against 14.8 to 47.1 mV for the file's own factor). ### 1.41 S9 step A: the 3RC-D model and its fit on the Mix runs

**The 3RC-D model is the 2RC-I model of 1.28 with two changes: a third RC branch with a fixed time constant tau3 = 45 s and a resistance table R3(T, SoC), and the slow branch's resistance multiplied by a scale table s(T, SoC), R2 = s · R2 of v1.0. R3 and s are fitted once, jointly over all six temperatures, by bounded linear least squares on the open-loop voltage of the 40 Mix runs. OCV, dOCV/dSoC, R0, the R1 table over current, tau1 and tau2 are the 2RC-I tables, unchanged. There is one fit; it does not run again after its report, whatever the report shows (1.40).**

- Reason for these two terms: the pulse test resolves the fast part of the model (R0, R1, tau1) and the C/20 and rest data give the OCV, so those stay. What the pulse test resolved poorly is everything slower than its 10 s pulses: R2 comes from one 1 h rest per level under the assumption V2 = 0 at the start of the move (1.12), and no branch covers 10 to 100 s (1.21, 1.25). Both terms act on the branch voltages only, so they vanish when the cell rests and do not move the OCV.
- Branch structure, time constant and node count chosen on the calibration set only (exploratory, leave-one-Mix-run-out, open loop on the 2RC-I error over the evaluation window, manual diagnostics of 2026-11-06; no model error of a standard cycle was read for these choices (one script printed the standard cycles' lowest reference SoC), and the numbers must not be quoted in the README):
  - The form below, fitted on 39 Mix runs and scored on the one left out, gives a mean RMSE of 8.8, 10.2, 15.2, 20.5, 31.8 and 47.6 mV at 40, 25, 10, 0, -10 and -20 degC against 12.5, 17.3, 25.0, 37.7, 62.1 and 80.6 mV for 2RC-I; no Mix run gets worse when left out. The exploratory fits differ from the fit below in three details: they applied s at the lookup point (the product of two interpolations, not the product on the grid), read tau2 at the end row of a step, and tied a node without data to its neighbour instead of leaving it at the 2RC-I value. In the review of this entry the first detail moved the in-sample means by at most 0.15 mV; the third changes no cost row, because the one tied node has no row.
  - tau3 = 45 s: in the form below, the left-out RMSE with 20 / 30 / 45 s is 9.2 / 8.8 / 8.8, 11.2 / 10.5 / 10.2, 17.0 / 15.9 / 15.2, 24.0 / 21.9 / 20.5, 37.9 / 34.5 / 31.8 and 53.1 / 49.7 / 47.6 mV at 40 to -20 degC, so 45 s is lowest or equal at every temperature. In fits one temperature at a time, with the R2 change as an added resistance, 60 s was within 0.4 mV of 45 s and better at no more than two temperatures. The pooled ceiling of 1.25 gave 20 to 30 s for a single constant branch on the 2RC tables with R2 unchanged; here R2 is fitted with it.
  - An OCV offset over SoC in place of the R2 change (fits one temperature at a time, tau3 = 30 s) gives a left-out RMSE within 1.2 mV of it at 40 to 0 degC, better at two of those temperatures and worse at two, and adds nothing to the branch alone at -10 and -20 degC. It does not vanish at rest, so the R2 scale is chosen.
  - A series resistance over |I| (same fits) lowers the left-out RMSE at -10 and -20 degC by 4 to 8 mV and leaves the error averaged over 300 s within 1.2 mV, and the slow error is what the filter turns into SoC error. It would also be a second current-dependent resistance next to R1 of 1.28. It is left out.
  - Seven SoC nodes instead of four (compared at tau3 = 30 s) lower the left-out RMSE by 1.5 mV at 25 degC and by 1.1 mV or less at 40, 10, 0 and -20 degC, raise it by 0.4 mV at -10 degC, and put six scale values below 0.2 instead of two. Four nodes are chosen.
- Unknowns: R3 and s on the nodes T in {40, 25, 10, 0, -10, -20} degC (the grid of 1.8) times SoC in {0.1, 0.4, 0.7, 1.0}: 24 values per table. A fifth SoC node at 0 is not fitted and holds the 2RC-I value (R3 = 0, s = 1). Between SoC nodes the value is linear in SoC. On the 101-point grid of 1.8, R3(T_j, SoC_g) is that piecewise-linear function, and R2(T_j, SoC_g) = s_j(SoC_g) · R2_v1.0(T_j, SoC_g), the product taken on the grid. The model reads both with Eq. 4, like every table. tau3 = 45 s at every grid point.
- Below SoC 0.1, R3 and the R2 scale s taper linearly toward the 2RC-I node values at 0. This extrapolation attenuates the fitted corrections outside the evaluation window (C14). The RC states retain their history. This is chosen knowing where the S1 gate is decided (exploratory, cached 2RC-I open loop, checked in the review of this entry): US06 at 25 degC scores 29.75 mV over all rows, 22.14 mV inside the evaluation window and 53.81 mV on the 6,596 rows after it (16.4 % of the rows, reference SoC 0.069 to 0.100, 53.7 % of the squared error, mean error -49.2 mV: the model already drops too much there). A flat extension would apply the full correction outside the fit window. Its effect on the tail error depends on both resistance changes and the current history. No 3RC-D table has been simulated on that run.
- Bounds: 0 <= R3 <= 0.3 Ohm and 0 <= s <= 3. The lower bounds keep both resistances non-negative; the upper bounds are about twice the largest value the exploration gave (R3 up to 0.131 Ohm, s up to 1.47) and are there to make a failed fit visible. Every value on a bound is listed in the report.
- Basis tables and support: each fitted node has a basis table on the 101-point grid: 1 at the node, 0 at the other SoC nodes of its temperature row (the node at SoC 0 included) and in every other row, linear in SoC between nodes. A node's support is the sum over the cost rows of its basis table read with Eq. 4 at the row's temperature and reference SoC. A node whose support is below 1,000 is not fitted in either table and holds the 2RC-I value (R3 = 0, s = 1), like the node at SoC 0. In the exploration this leaves out one node: SoC 0.1 at -20 degC, where the Mix runs end at SoC 0.429 or above.
- Fit: one bounded linear least-squares problem for the fitted node values of both tables together, solved by `scipy.optimize.lsq_linear(X, y, bounds=(lower, upper), method='bvls')` with default tolerances. BVLS solves the convex problem to numerical tolerance. Require solver success and finite coefficients and optimality, and report status, success and first-order optimality. A failed solve is a numerical failure. In the review the default method gave values that differ in the sixth significant digit. A value counts as on a bound when its entry of the solver's `active_mask` is not zero.
  - Runs: the 40 Mix runs of 1.40. Rows of the cost: rows 0, 10, 20, ... of each run's evaluation window (C14), counted from the run's first row used, unweighted. The regressors are computed on every row; only the cost is thinned, because the error is strongly correlated over 1 s, so the full matrix would be ten times larger for no gain.
  - Error: the open-loop model as in 1.25 (`simulate_open_loop`, the reference SoC and the measured cell temperature as inputs, every branch voltage 0 at the first row), model minus measured voltage. No offset is fitted, per run or otherwise.
  - Linearity: with tau2 and tau3 fixed, V2 and V3 of Eq. 1 are linear in the resistance read at each step, Eq. 4 is linear in the table, and the tables are linear in the node values. So the model voltage is the 2RC-I voltage plus a linear function of the R3 values and of the values of s - 1. The regressor of a node's R3 is minus the voltage of Eq. 1 with tau3 and that node's basis table as the resistance table. The regressor of a node's s - 1 is minus the voltage of Eq. 1 with the v1.0 tau2 table and, as the resistance table, the basis table times the v1.0 R2 table (the product on the grid).   - In the filter, with the coefficients fitted on all Mix runs (in sample, which favours them), the experiment-1 Mix mean moves from 1.2736 to 1.2589 pp with the R0 increment and to 1.2795 pp with the R1 increment, and -20 degC gets worse with both; Mix 8 at -10 degC rises from 6.77 to 7.37 pp with the R1 increment. The gate of 1.52 gave 1.1362 pp on the same runs.
- Reading. Half of the limit-row error is a function of the model's own variables, and a table can take it; that half barely moves the SoC estimate. The other half is tied to the voltage limit itself: when the cycler holds 4.2 V it regulates the current, and the measured current and voltage on those rows do not follow the model at any SoC, current or history. Whether that is the cell, the cycler's regulation or its sampling cannot be told from this data. A model fitted to it would key on the measured voltage being at the limit, which is the gate of 1.52 under another name.
- What remains, as a limitation and future work. In the cold the experiment-1 SoC error of 3RC-D0 moves by about 3 to 5 pp per run across these intervals (`results/regen_limit.md`), in both directions. A rule for measurements at a charge-voltage limit would need its own entry that states up front how it treats a wrong start, the cost that made the gate fail guard 6.
- Process: entries 1.48 to 1.50 were written before the S11 runs they govern but were not committed before them; the commit order records them first.
